The History of the Volkswagen Beetle

The Volkswagen Beetle, affectionately known as the "Bug," is one of the most iconic cars in automotive history. Designed in the 1930s by Ferdinand Porsche, the Beetle was intended to be a "people's car" (Volkswagen in German) that was affordable, reliable, and efficient.

Production began in 1938, and the Beetle quickly became a symbol of post-war recovery and innovation. Its unique design, with a rear-mounted engine and rounded shape, made it instantly recognizable. Over the decades, the Beetle evolved, but it retained its charm and popularity, becoming a cultural icon in the 1960s and 1970s.

By the time production ended in 2019, over 21 million Beetles had been sold worldwide, cementing its legacy as one of the best-selling cars of all time.
